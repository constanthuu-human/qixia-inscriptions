Buddhist legends in Nanjing

Stories about relics, crossings, flowers, appearances, and images helped make Nanjing’s Buddhist landscape sacred.

Stories that made Nanjing sacred

A legend is also historical evidence.

Legends are not simple administrative records. They show how communities explained sacred power, connected places to famous figures, and gave later visitors a way to read the landscape.

01 · Jianchu Temple and the Changgan area · 建初寺与长干里

Kang Senghui and the miraculous relic 康僧会感得舍利

Relics, faith, and the public arrival of Buddhism · 舍利、信仰与佛教进入公共空间

Later reliquary image connected to Nanjing relic worship; not the legendary Eastern Wu relic itself · 后世舍利容器图像，与南京舍利崇拜相关，并非传说中的东吴舍利实物。

The legend

Kang Senghui arrived in the Eastern Wu capital when Buddhism was still unfamiliar in the lower Yangzi world. Sun Quan asked him to show that the new religion possessed real spiritual power. Kang and his followers purified themselves, burned incense, and prayed over a bronze vessel. Nothing appeared during the first seven days or the second. Near the end of the third period, a sound came from the vessel and a relic was found inside. The ruler tested it by striking and burning it; the relic remained unharmed. Sun Quan then ordered a pagoda and a temple, giving the story a political ending: a travelling monk became the beginning of a public Buddhist institution.

康僧会来到东吴都城时，佛教在长江下游仍是一种陌生的宗教。孙权要求他证明这种新宗教确有灵验。康僧会与弟子净身、焚香，对着铜瓶祈祷。第一个七日、第二个七日都没有结果。第三个七日将尽时，铜瓶中传出声响，瓶内出现舍利。孙权用敲击、火烧等方式试验，舍利仍然不坏。于是孙权下令建塔建寺，故事也以一种政治性的结果结束：一位远来的僧人，被记忆为公共佛教制度的开端。

Buddhist meaning

The relic represents the Buddha’s continuing presence after physical death. It also turns a religious claim into a public encounter: a monk, a ruler, a test, and an institution are brought into one scene. The story is not interesting because a miracle can be proved; it is interesting because it explains why Jianchu Temple deserved authority in the memory of southern Buddhism.

舍利象征佛陀在肉身灭度之后仍然持续存在。它也把宗教主张转化为公共相遇：僧人、君主、试验与寺院制度被放进同一个场景。这个故事的意义不在于证明神迹，而在于解释建初寺为什么能够在南方佛教记忆中拥有开端性的权威。

Historical note

The story is preserved in medieval Buddhist biographies and miracle records, not in a surviving Eastern Wu court report or an archaeological object that verifies the event. Jianchu Temple and the Changgan Buddhist landscape are historically important, but the vessel, the tests, and the miraculous appearance belong to religious memory.

这个故事保存在中古佛教传记与感应记录中，而不是一份留存至今的东吴宫廷报告，也不是一件能够直接验证事件的考古器物。建初寺与长干佛教景观具有历史重要性，但铜瓶、试验和舍利出现的情节属于宗教记忆。

02 · Liang court, Mufu Mountain, and the Yangtze · 梁廷、幕府山与长江

Bodhidharma crosses the Yangtze on a reed 达摩一苇渡江

Good deeds are not identical to awakening · 功德不等同于觉悟

Later artistic representation of the legend; not an eyewitness record · 后世艺术表现，并非目击记录。

The legend

Bodhidharma met Emperor Wu of Liang in Jiankang. The emperor listed the temples he had built, the monks he had supported, the scriptures he had copied, and the ceremonies he had sponsored. He expected these deeds to reveal a vast store of merit. In the later Chan encounter, Bodhidharma answered that such works were beneficial but did not by themselves amount to awakening. The conversation failed. Bodhidharma left the court, went toward the Yangtze, and—when pursuit followed—broke a reed from the riverbank and crossed to the northern shore. Mufu Mountain preserves a local landscape for the story, even though the image of the reed belongs to later religious imagination.

达摩在建康与梁武帝相遇。皇帝列举自己修建的寺院、供养的僧人、抄写的经书和举行的法会，期待这些功德证明自己已经拥有巨大的宗教成就。在后来的禅宗相遇故事中，达摩回答说，这些行为虽然有益，却不能自动等同于觉悟。两人没有达成共识。达摩离开宫廷，走向长江；追赶者随后赶来，他便从江边折下一枝芦苇，渡到北岸。幕府山保存了这一故事的地方记忆，但“一苇”的形象属于后世宗教想象。

Buddhist meaning

The reed is a sharp contrast to the Liang emperor’s monumental Buddhism. Temples and ceremonies can help a society, but awakening cannot be counted like a building project. The legend turns a political disagreement into a Buddhist lesson about the limits of visible achievement.

芦苇与梁武帝宏大的帝国佛教形成鲜明对比。寺院和法会可以帮助社会，但觉悟不能像工程数量一样计算。这个传说把一次政治性的失和，转化成关于外在成就边界的佛教教训。

Historical note

The meeting between Bodhidharma and Emperor Wu, and especially the reed crossing, are later Chan traditions. The encounter is not a contemporary Liang court transcript. The legend’s historical value lies in how later communities used the Liang court and the Yangtze landscape to explain Chan independence from political patronage.

达摩与梁武帝相遇，以及尤其著名的一苇渡江，都是后出的禅宗传统，并非同时代梁廷实录。这个传说的历史价值在于：后来的僧团借助梁廷与长江景观，解释禅宗如何保持对政治赞助的独立性。

03 · Niushou Mountain and the Oxhead tradition · 牛首山与牛头禅传统

Farong and the birds carrying flowers 法融与衔花之鸟

Meditation and freedom from attachment to miracles · 禅修与不执着于神迹

Open-licensed photograph of Mount Niushou, the landscape associated with Farong’s tradition; it does not depict the flower miracle itself · 牛首山开放许可照片，表现法融传说所关联的山林景观，并非鸟衔花神迹本身。

The legend

Farong meditated in the mountains of Niushou. Animals were said to become calm around his dwelling: deer approached, dangerous creatures did not attack, and birds carried flowers in their beaks and placed them before him. At first, the flowers seem to prove that Farong had become extraordinary. A later Chan interpretation reverses the scene. After Farong received deeper instruction and no longer clung to his own spiritual achievement, the birds stopped coming. The quiet mountain then became more important than the spectacle.

法融在牛首山中修行。传说动物在他的住处附近变得安静：鹿前来亲近，猛兽不再伤害他，鸟雀还衔着花朵放在他面前。起初，花朵似乎证明法融已经成为非凡的圣者。后来的禅宗解释却把故事倒转过来：法融受到更深的教导、不再执着于自己的修行成就之后，鸟雀便不再衔花。于是，安静的山林比奇观本身更重要。

Buddhist meaning

The disappearance of the flowers is the point. A miracle can become another object of attachment, another way for the practitioner to imagine a special self. When the sign disappears, practice is no longer dependent on recognition from birds, followers, or the landscape.

花朵消失才是故事的重点。神迹也可能变成新的执着，使修行者继续想象一个特殊的自我。当神迹不再出现，修行便不再依靠鸟雀、弟子或自然景观的认可。

Historical note

Farong and the Niutou community are historically documented through monk biographies and later Chan transmission materials. The birds carrying flowers are a hagiographic motif. Even within the tradition, the story changes meaning over time: it moves from proof of sanctity to a warning against needing proof.

法融与牛头共同体通过僧传和后来的禅宗传灯材料而被记载。鸟雀衔花属于神异传记母题。即使在传统内部，这个故事也经历了意义变化：它从圣迹的证明，转变为对“需要证明”这一心理的提醒。

04 · Zhongshan and the later Linggu Temple landscape · 钟山与后来的灵谷寺景观

Baozhi appears in several places 宝志分身多处

Holiness cannot always be judged by outward behaviour · 神圣性不总能从外表判断

Later printed representation, not a contemporary portrait · 后世刻本画像，并非同时代肖像。

The legend

Baozhi was remembered as an unconventional monk: barefoot, carrying a staff fitted with strange objects, speaking in difficult phrases, and moving between ordinary people and the Liang court. One story says that different communities claimed he had stayed with them on the same night. Messengers went to investigate, but people in several places each insisted that Baozhi had truly been there. The legend turned a difficult social fact—a charismatic monk whose influence travelled widely—into a story of appearing in many bodies at once. After his death, Emperor Wu had him buried at Zhongshan and established a memorial religious site that later memory connected with the Linggu landscape.

宝志被记忆为一位不寻常的僧人：赤足行走，手持装有奇异物件的锡杖，说出难以理解的话语，并往来于普通民众与梁廷之间。有一个故事说，不同地方的寺院都声称宝志在同一夜曾经住过。使者前往查问，却发现各地的人都坚持说宝志确实来过。这个传说把一个难以解释的社会事实——一位魅力型僧人的影响力可以远距离传播——转化成了“同时出现在多处”的神异故事。宝志去世后，梁武帝把他葬在钟山并建立纪念性宗教场所，后世又把它与灵谷寺景观联系起来。

Buddhist meaning

The story asks the audience not to confuse respectability with spiritual value. Baozhi’s oddity gives him a space outside ordinary official behaviour. His many appearances also express a devotional idea: a compassionate figure is not limited to one audience or one room.

这个故事要求观者不要把“体面”与宗教价值混为一谈。宝志的怪异使他获得了普通官员无法拥有的言说空间。他分身多处的故事也表达了一种信仰观念：慈悲的宗教人物不被一个听众或一间房屋限制。

Historical note

Baozhi is historically associated with the Southern Dynasties court and Zhongshan, but the multiple-appearance story belongs to sacred biography. The burial and memorial landscape are historical claims about patronage and place; simultaneous appearances are not independently verifiable events.

宝志与南朝宫廷及钟山之间的联系具有历史依据，但分身多处的故事属于神异传记。葬地与纪念性寺院是关于赞助和地点的历史主张；同时出现在多处则不能作为独立核验的事件。

05 · Jiming Temple · 鸡鸣寺

Why Guanyin sits facing backward 菩萨为何倒坐

Compassion and moral self-reflection · 慈悲与自我反省

A site photograph of the Jiming Temple complex and its Guanyin Hall setting; the reverse-facing image itself is not visible · 鸡鸣寺建筑群及观音殿环境照片，画面中看不到倒坐观音本身。

The legend

Jiming Temple is associated with a Guanyin image that faces north rather than toward the usual front of the hall. The temple saying explains the unusual direction: “Why does the bodhisattva sit facing backward? Because living beings refuse to turn back.” The line makes the statue’s orientation into a small drama. Guanyin is not turning away in anger. The bodhisattva faces those who have continued in the wrong direction, while the visitor is asked whether they are willing to stop and turn around.

鸡鸣寺与一尊面向北方、没有朝向通常殿门方向的观音像联系在一起。寺院楹联解释了这一异常朝向：“问菩萨为何倒坐，叹众生不肯回头。”这句话把佛像的方向变成一场小型戏剧。观音并不是因为愤怒而背对众生，而是面向那些已经走错方向的人；观者也被问到：自己是否愿意停下来，回头改变方向。

Buddhist meaning

The legend turns compassion into an active orientation. Guanyin is not simply waiting to be worshipped from the correct side; the image is imagined as already facing the people who need to change. The physical turn and the moral turn become one idea.

这个传说把慈悲表现为主动的朝向。观音并不是只等待人们从正确的一面来礼拜；在传说中，菩萨已经转向那些需要改变的人。身体的转身与道德上的回头因此成为同一个观念。

Historical note

The temple’s reverse-facing Guanyin and its couplet are part of Jiming Temple’s living religious tradition. The exterior photograph cannot establish the statue’s exact date, materials, repairs, crown, objects, or hand gesture. Those details should remain open until a clear interior image or temple record is available.

倒坐观音及其楹联属于鸡鸣寺持续存在的宗教传统。寺院外景照片不能证明观音像的确切年代、材质、修补、冠饰、持物或手印。除非获得清晰的殿内照片或寺院记录，否则这些细节应保持开放。
